Suggested title: **Why your tablet budget should include the pen**

---

Most students compare tablets by the price on the box. For handwritten notes
that number is misleading, because the tablet is only half of the setup. The
other half is an active pen that the manufacturer lists as compatible with that
exact model, and on many tablets it is sold separately.

## A stylus is not always a pen

A cheap capacitive stylus behaves like a fingertip. The screen cannot tell it
apart from your palm, so resting your hand while you write leaves stray marks.
An active pen communicates with the display, which is what makes palm rejection
possible. Pens are rarely interchangeable across brands, and sometimes not
across models from the same brand, so check the compatibility list before
buying.

## The price gap is real

In India, some tablets include their pen in the box, while others charge
several thousand rupees for it. A tablet that looks cheaper on its own can end
up costing more than one with the pen included. When you compare, add the pen
to the tablet price first and compare the totals.

## Palm rejection depends on the app too

Palm rejection comes from the pen, the tablet and the note app working
together. Reviewers often find it reliable for writing and less consistent in
drawing apps, where a resting hand can trigger gestures like two-finger undo.
If your note app has a setting to draw with the pen only, turn it on.

## A simple way to compare

Decide three things before you shop: whether you need a pen now or later, your
total budget including that pen, and whether you need a particular platform for
a course app. Then compare only the setups that meet all three.
